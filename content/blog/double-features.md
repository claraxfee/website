### Ocean’s Eleven (2001, dir. Steven Soderbergh) / Burn After Reading (2008, dir. Coen Brothers)

Two very excellent, very funny, fast-paced movies featuring both George Clooney and Brad Pitt. Sort of spy-ish heist theme here. Ocean's Eleven comes first because it's not as good as Burn After Reading (sorry) and because it's both older and more recognizable. Good for all ages, sort of! Perfect for showing the tween you're babysitting so they can feel like they're getting away with something.

### A Woman Under the Influence (1975, dir. John Cassavetes) / Women on the Verge of a Nervous Breakdown (1988, dir. Pedro Almodóvar)

For no other reason than the titles. Women on the Verge second to relieve the tension created by the first movie. This is a good one for a party, ideally one where you serve gazpacho.

### Annihilation (2018, dir. Alex Garland) / Stalker (1979, dir. Andrei Tarkofsky)

Two movies about a mysterious Zone in which things get weird. Annihilation was most definitely inspired by Stalker, though the director (and the author of the book it was based on) claim otherwise. (One [Letterboxd review](https://letterboxd.com/sarahsquirm/film/annihilation/#comments) of Annihilation simply reads, "girl Stalker".)

Annihilation is a personal favorite: great special effects, Natalie Portman, an incredible score, sci-fi with an fascinating premise. In the Zone the laws of biology do not apply; plant, animal, and human mix and fester and overgrow. An allegorical treatment of the inexplicably self-destructive nature of cancer; in Annihilation, death is not a conclusion but a change. This is standard but excellently executed sci-fi fodder, from the director of Ex Machina. Unfortunately the first 75% of this movie is not nearly as good as the latter quarter. What can you do!

Stalker is a much better movie, but both are worth watching. In this Zone, laws of science are similarly inapplicable, and both those who enter and those who live near it are fundamentally and eerily changed. At its center is a myseterious room that will grant your heart's desire. (An aside: this movie predated Chernobyl by 7 years!) A ragtag band---three men who go by their titles, e.g. the Writer and the Professor, as in Annihilation---journey into its depths in search of that room. Insanely beautiful cinematography, incredible ambient score, with a slow, meditative pace that (I swear!) never bores. An indescribably alien world without a hint of special effects. 

My favorite illuminating anecdote about Stalker: this movie gave several people cancer. It was shot downstream of abandoned chemical and power plants; there are shots of ["snow falling in summer and white foam floating down the river"](https://web.archive.org/web/20180322094135/https://people.ucalgary.ca/~tstronds/nostalghia.com/TheTopics/Stalker/sharun.html). Several people involved in its production, including the director, died from cancer in the years following.

### Blade Runner (1982, dir. Ridley Scott) / Ghost in the Shell (1995, dir. Mamoru Oshii)

More sci-fi and more personal favorites. Blade Runner first because it's the (visual) inspirational force for Ghost in the Shell. Two movies that deal with identity and humanity as technology blurs the human and the machine; excellence in the "cyberpunk" genre. Ghost in the Shell is mellower, less gritty, and perhaps more beautiful. This is the kind of movie that spawned a thousand imitations, such that you might first note its cheap imitations before remembering that it came first. Often imitated, never replicated! 

Blade Runner is more action-y, more straightforwardly metaphorical, and unfortunately a significantly worse movie. It's boringly paced and features cringy scenes, like Harrison Ford's character raping another. Ghost in the Shell is the personal favorite, not this, for the record. Worth watching for the visuals alone, though; it was blueprint for nearly every dystopian-sci-fi-ish movie that came after.

### Tampopo (1985, dir. Juzo Itami) / Eat Drink Man Woman (1994, dir. Ang Lee)

Two excellent movies that revolve around food, especially the rituals and social functions of cooking food. One Chinese, the other Japanese; one significantly more fun and wacky than heartwarming; both featuring perhaps the best cooking montages ever put to screen. Also an excellent pairing for a dinner party. Serve dinner in between; Tampopo first because it WILL make you hungry.
